# Family Wealth Intelligence: 12-month business plan

**Status:** working plan for the CEO. All prices, volumes and dates are assumptions to test, not forecasts.

## 1. Business in one paragraph

Family Wealth Intelligence (FWI) is an information business for the lawyers, tax advisers, trustees, wealth managers, family offices and other advisers who serve wealthy families in the UK, Europe, US and Asia. It earns money from the advisers it serves, not from consumers: **sponsored directory listings, newsletter and display sponsorship, sponsored thought leadership, paid reports and rankings, CPD webinars, a premium data subscription, and events.** The site is built to be revenue-ready on day one: every page carries a product (see `docs/RATE-CARD.md`).

**Focus rule:** tag every piece of content by region from day one, but build sales depth in the UK first. Selling in four regions at once dilutes focus.

## 3. Revenue stack (in launch order)

1. Sponsored directory listings (Free, Featured, Premium). Needs little audience; the claim flow is the main upgrade route.
2. Newsletter sponsorship and display ads.
3. Sponsored content and thought leadership (always labelled).
4. Paid reports and rankings.
5. CPD webinars.
6. Premium subscription (data access and alerts), once the databases are worth paying for.
7. Events: sponsorship plus tickets. The margin engine in year two, not launch revenue.

### Assumptions behind the scenario

- **Directory:** Featured £1,500 and Premium £4,500 a year. Founding partners (6 Featured, 2 Premium) pay 30% less for an annual commitment. New paying listings per quarter: Q2 10 Featured and 2 Premium; Q3 14 and 4; Q4 20 and 6. This is roughly 3% to 5% of 500+ listed firms upgrading by month 12.
- **Newsletter:** one primary sponsor slot at £1,200 per issue and two classified slots at £400 per issue, half filled. Weekly issues sold: Q2 6, Q3 10, Q4 12.
- **Display:** £35 CPM, 40% of impressions sold; page views of 40,000, 120,000 and 250,000 a quarter in Q2 to Q4.
- **Sponsored content:** £3,500 each; 2, 5 and 8 in Q2 to Q4.
- **Reports and rankings:** £8,000 sponsorship; 1, 1 and 2 in Q2 to Q4.
- **CPD webinars:** £2,500 sponsorship; 2, 4 and 4.
- **Subscriptions:** Professional £395 a year (40 in Q3, 80 in Q4); Firm licence £2,500 a year (1 in Q3, 3 in Q4).
- **Events:** two roundtables at £8,000 host fee (Q3, Q4); summit with six sponsors averaging £8,000 and 70 paid tickets at £450. Ticket price is a placeholder until the venue is chosen.

### Costs to budget (not modelled here)

Fill these before relying on the scenario: editorial review time (legal and tax sign-off), tooling (hosting, database, email platform, AI drafting API), design, sales time or commission, event venue and delegate costs, CPD accreditation fees, insurance, and accountancy. A directory-led model has low variable cost until events start; events carry real cash risk before tickets and sponsors are sold.

## 6. Priorities for revenue-readiness

1. **Pre-sell founding partnerships before launch.** Directory and newsletter sponsorship need little audience to start.
2. **Build the email list from day one.** The newsletter sign-up is on every page.
3. **Claim outreach is the engine.** Every unclaimed profile is a lead. Work the list in order of firm size and sector fit.
4. **Treat events as the margin engine in year two.** Use webinars and roundtables to prove demand first.
5. **Never blur editorial and paid.** Sponsored content is always labelled; legal and tax stories always get human sign-off.

## 7. KPIs to review weekly

Newsletter subscribers and open rate; profiles claimed; claim-to-paid conversion; sponsor pipeline value; sponsored issues sold; webinar registrations; subscriber count and churn; articles published versus drafted (editorial throughput).

## 8. Risks and how the plan handles them

| Risk | Handling |
|---|---|
| Agent-written content is wrong | Every draft lands as pending review; legal and tax always signed off by a person; the pipeline may only use supplied source text |
| Thin audience at launch | Sell directory and founding-partner deals first; they need little audience |
| Four-region sprawl | UK first; Europe from month 7; US and Asia from month 10 |
| Sponsors expect editorial influence | Labelled sponsored content only; written editorial policy |
| Event loses money | Pre-sell sponsors before committing to venue; start with webinars and roundtables |
| Infrastructure pauses (free database tier) | Upgrade the database plan before public launch; the site is static so reading stays up |
